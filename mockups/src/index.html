Website redesign · for board review

Five directions for the
Friends of Hendricks site.

Each is a complete design of the same two pages — a home page and the Business Partner Program page. All three use the colors from the Friends of Hendricks logo, so the choice is about voice and audience, not brand.

Before you look: every price, name, date and sponsor on these pages is real, taken from the current site and the 2025–26 partner flyer. Where a figure does not exist anywhere — total raised to date, for instance — the space is marked “needs real figure” rather than filled with a plausible-looking number. Photography is the one real gap: only two usable photos exist, so the same Walkathon picture appears in all three.

The options

Pick a voice.

A

Standing Strong

Restrained and editorial. Aimed at business sponsors and larger gifts.

How it looks

Newsreader serif headlines with Archivo body. The logo is already set in a serif, so this is the closest of the three to the existing mark.

What it does differently

  • Full-width photograph with the text set beside it, lots of white space
  • Red used only on buttons — nowhere else
  • Impact figures set large in the serif
  • A curved rule between sections, drawn from the open book in the logo
  • Sponsorship shown as a full comparison table, for someone weighing levels

B

Schoolyard

Warm and rounded. Aimed at parents, sign-ups and turnout.

How it looks

Caprasimo display over Assistant body — the same pairing Kinder Ground uses, and both are free. The gold comes from the Friends of Hendricks flyer, not from an inspiration site.

What it does differently

  • Rounded cards, soft shadows, curved dividers between sections
  • Prices shown as stickers you could peel off
  • A hand-drawn underline on the phrase that matters
  • The bell from the logo reused as a small repeating mark
  • Sponsorship shown as five choose-your-level cards, with the full table tucked behind a toggle

C

Momentum

Bold and urgent. Built around the February Walkathon.

How it looks

Anton in oversized capitals with Barlow Condensed beneath. The loudest of the three by a distance.

What it does differently

  • Full-width blocks of solid navy and red, one after another
  • A live countdown to Saturday 20 February 2027
  • The $50,000 goal shown as a fundraising bar, borrowed from their own flyer thermometer
  • A donate button fixed to the bottom of the screen on phones
  • Sponsorship shown as five full-width slabs with the price set huge

D

Reel

Cinematic and dark. Film is the whole idea, not a decoration.

How it looks

Bricolage Grotesque over Manrope. Deliberately dark in every theme — the way a cinema is dark — rather than a washed-out light version of a design that wants the lights down.

What it does differently

  • A full-screen moving hero, and two more further down the page
  • Very few words. The picture carries the argument
  • Sections fade in as you reach them
  • A motion on/off control, and it starts paused for anyone who asked their system to reduce motion
  • Sponsorship as hairline rows with the price set large and thin

E

Foundation

The closest analog to obama.org. Institutional, warm, film at the top.

How it looks

Figtree for headlines, Source Serif 4 for the quotes, Archivo Narrow for labels — standing in for Gotham, Chronicle Text and Gotham Condensed. Uses the Obama Foundation's own warm off-whites as neutrals; every accent is still yours.

What it does differently

  • Sans headlines, not serif — which is what Obama actually does, and where direction A departs from it
  • Film hero with the words in the bottom corner
  • Square picture cards with the text underneath — their core grid
  • Calls to action as underlined links, not filled buttons: navigation rather than a hard sell
  • One phrase repeated as a divider, the way they repeat theirs. Here it is the line already on your logo

About the motion in D and E. What moves on those pages is not video — it is the one real Walkathon photograph, slowly scaled and drifted in CSS. It adds no file at all, and real footage swaps straight in later. Each has a control to stop it. The separate video options page covers what real footage would weigh, what it would cost to host, and the consent question — which is the part that needs a board decision rather than a design one.

The same in all three

What you get whichever you pick.

Prices you can read

All five sponsorship levels and nine benefits become real text. Today they exist only inside a flat image — unreadable on a phone, invisible to Google, silent to a screen reader.

The right email address

All five program inboxes published where they belong. The current Contact page lists none of them.

Works on a phone

Nothing scrolls sideways. The sponsorship table folds down into readable cards rather than shrinking.

Light and dark

Both designed deliberately, and every text color checked to WCAG AA contrast in each.

Facts nobody was using

The $50,000 goal, the 1942 founding year, the student-designed Walkathon shirt, the full board, all 49 sponsors.

No stale content

The 2021 placeholder post and the comment boxes sitting under the payment form are gone.

What we need from the board

Six things, whichever wins.

Which of the five?

The only decision needed today. If none is quite right, say which parts of which and I will combine them.

Is the Walkathon the 20th or the 21st of February?

The sponsorship chart says both — the header says Saturday the 20th, the booth row says the 21st. In 2027 the 20th is the Saturday.

Photographs, and permission to use them

The biggest gap. Only two usable photos exist and most program images are 300 pixels square. Pictures of identifiable children need signed photo-release consent from the school before they go on a public site.

A total raised, and what it bought

The $50,000 Walkathon goal is the only figure published anywhere. A cumulative total, and one or two specific things it paid for, would carry real weight with sponsors.

The logo as a vector file

Only an 868×1024 pixel image exists. An SVG, AI or EPS would let the mark print and scale cleanly.

Confirmed sponsor names

A handful of Legacy and Investors Circle businesses are currently identifiable only by their logo file name.
