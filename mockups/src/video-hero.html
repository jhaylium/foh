If you want real footage

Four things, in order of difficulty.

Requirements for a real video hero
WhatDetailWho
Signed consent Video release from the school for every identifiable child on camera. Stricter in practice than stills, because a face on film is harder to argue is incidental. Settle this before anyone shoots.Board and school
Footage A modern phone is genuinely fine. Ten to fifteen seconds, held steady or on a slow pan, shot horizontally. Wide shots of the crowd walking carry the idea better than close-ups of individual faces — and sidestep much of the consent problem. Any parent volunteer
Preparation Trim to a loop, strip the audio, export WebM and MP4 at 1280px, and cut a poster frame for the first paint and for reduced-motion visitors.Me, about an hour
Wiring it in One element swap. The markup is already in this page as a comment, ready to uncomment.Me, minutes
The consent point is the real one

Everything else here is a small job. Putting moving pictures of named children on a public website is the part that deserves a board decision rather than a design decision, and it is worth asking the school what its existing release forms already cover before any filming happens.

  • Wide crowd shots need far less paperwork than faces.
  • Footage of the event — feet walking, banners, the finish, the Spirit Rock — carries the feeling with nobody identifiable at all.
  • Whatever you shoot, the video must stay silent and decorative, so a visitor who cannot see it loses nothing.

On which direction suits it: you are right that Momentum handles full-bleed motion best — it is already built from edge-to-edge blocks. But Standing Strong is the direction actually derived from the Obama Foundation, and treatment two above is its layout. Either works.
